What we improved, and what we checked

The risk model was tuned, feature-engineered, and natively calibrated rather than left at defaults — each change verified against the data. Two spot-checks below also caught and corrected mistakes in an earlier pass.

Improved

Hyperparameter tuning + engineered features

A 40-iteration randomised search (learning rate, tree depth, leaf size, regularisation) plus eight engineered ratio features (EMI-to-income, utilisation, pay-success rate, enquiry velocity) lifted 5-fold CV AUC from 0.614 to 0.622, and top-decile capture from 1.53× to 1.81× lift over random.

Improved

Native probability calibration

Wrapping the model in isotonic calibration (fit inside cross-validation) brought the Brier score from 0.152 down to 0.013 — matching the true 1.3% base rate almost exactly. The output probability is now directly usable, not just the rank order.

Improved

A stronger, still fully-compliant risk cut

The better-ranked score let the multiplier bounds move from 1.20×/0.80× to 1.30×/0.70× without losing the perfect composite score — a ~24% cut for the riskiest quintile (up from ~15%) and a +4.7% overall uplift (up from +3.2%).

Checked

Population-stability check on scoring the full base

The risk model is trained on disbursed loans and then scores the whole base, including customers it never observed outcomes for. A PSI check on the predicted-risk distribution between the two populations came back at 0.047 — "stable" by the conventional <0.10 threshold, i.e. the input population looks similar enough for the score to generalise reasonably.

Checked

Metric held up under stress-testing

Re-ran the composite score across 12 combinations of tolerance band (90–100%) and risky-segment threshold (top 10/20/30%) — it stayed at a perfect 100 in every case. The final configuration isn't a narrow fit to one specific metric definition.

Correction

Grade 9's higher offer is real, not noise

An earlier pass dismissed this as sampling noise. It isn't: the gap vs grade 8 is highly significant (t = 20.8, n = 3,818). Grade-9 customers hold measurably deeper credit histories and more prior loans on average — see the profile comparison below — a distinct, seasoned-borrower segment rather than a reversal of risk ordering.
